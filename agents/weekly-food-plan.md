# Weekly food plan

Runs Friday at 12:45 a.m. Unattended. Reads three files from the family computer, writes a PDF and appends one of the files, fills a grocery cart in the browser, and never checks out.

---

WEEKLY FOOD PLAN for [Household]. This runs early Friday morning. Do the whole job without asking questions (nobody is awake). At the end, tell [Person] what you decided anywhere you had to make a judgment call.

WRITING RULE: in every message and in the PDF, never use em dashes or hyphens used as dashes. Use commas, periods, or reworded sentences.

STORAGE RULE: do not use cloud storage for this task. Files live only on the family computer, in the folder [path to Weekly Food Plan] (in the device shell this is `$HOME/mnt/<folder>/Weekly Food Plan`). Use the device file tools to read and write there.

## Before planning

Read `plan-history.txt` in the folder, if it exists. It lists past weeks' dinners. Do not repeat dinners from the last 3 or 4 weeks; bringing back clear favorites now and then is fine.

Read `favorite-recipes.txt`. It is the household's collection of loved recipes, already adapted to the food rules below. Build at least 2 of each week's dinners or lunches from it, rotating through the list and checking `plan-history.txt` so the same ones do not repeat within 3 or 4 weeks. Credit the source shown in the file in the PDF. If new recipes are added to the file, include them in the rotation.

Read `fridge-leftovers.txt` if it exists. It was written on Thursday evening. Plan the first days of the week around using those items up, and say in the summary which ones you used.

## What to deliver

1. A meal plan from Friday dinner through the following Thursday: breakfast, lunch, dinner, snacks, and the Saturday morning bake.
2. A PDF with every recipe (ingredients with amounts, steps, servings), the day and meal it is for, and how long its leftovers keep in the fridge (and freezer where that applies), using USDA and FoodSafety.gov guidance. Cooked leftovers are generally 3 to 4 days, so if a leftover would be eaten past its safe window, say so or move it. Include macros per adult serving and a daily total per adult for calories, protein, carbs, fat, and fiber. Add a short line on the key micronutrients each day covers. Include a consolidated ingredient list grouped by store section. Make it clean and easy to read on a phone.
3. Every ingredient added to the [store] cart in the browser (details below). DO NOT CHECK OUT, ever. A person checks out because they pick the pickup time.
4. Save the PDF to the folder as "Food plan week of YYYY-MM-DD.pdf" (that Friday's date). Then append this week's date and dinner list to `plan-history.txt` (create it if missing). Confirm the PDF landed by listing the folder.
5. Send the PDF and a short summary: the week's menu at a glance, what went in the cart, anything out of stock or substituted, the "check your pantry" list, the cart's estimated total if the store shows one, and where the PDF was saved.

Coffee, tea, flour, oil, butter, spices, seeds, honey, baking basics, and any other shelf-stable staple a recipe needs that the household likely has. Add a staple to the cart only if the plan needs an unusual one or a large amount.

## Recipe sources

[Two or three sites or newsletters the household likes, with the instruction to browse them each week, pull at least 2 or 3 recipes adapted to the rules, link the source in the PDF, and write the recipes in your own words rather than copying.] Give each dinner a one-line description that could be used as a social caption.

## Store cart (browser)

1. Read the browser skill first, then load the browser tools in one call. Use batched actions to move faster.
2. Open a new tab at the store's site. The account is signed in and the store location and pickup setting are set. Do not change the store, the pickup or delivery setting, or any account settings.
3. If the store is not signed in, or the browser is not reachable, do not try to sign in. Still make the plan and the PDF, then say the cart was not filled and why, and put the full shopping list in the message.
4. Look at what is already in the cart. Do not remove anything a person added. Do not add duplicates.
5. Search for each item and add the right quantity for the recipes. Prefer the household's packaging and ingredient preferences. If an item is out of stock, choose the closest good substitute and note it.
6. Never press Checkout, Place Order, or anything that submits the order or charges a card. Never enter payment details. Do not clip coupons or change anything else on the account.
7. When done, read the cart back to confirm everything landed, then close the tabs you opened.

## Finish

Send the PDF and the summary. If anything failed (browser, store, or saving to the computer), say exactly what and what the person needs to do.
